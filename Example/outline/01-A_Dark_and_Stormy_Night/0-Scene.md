It was a dark and stormy night.  The rain fell in torrents--except at occasional intervals, when it was checked by a violent gust of wind that rattled the shutters of the old harbor house..

Mabel pressed her nose to the window and read the *Gazette’s* headline for the third time.She had lived through the great storms of 1887-1889, and none of them had sounded quite like this…

“Someone is at the door.” Mabel whispered. “Did you hear that?.”

“Who's there” Harold asked, not looking up from the Gazette.

“Then the wind has learned to knock”, she said.

“It’s only the wind,” Harold said , turning the page. “Go back to bed.



The candle guttered. 
Somewhere below - far below - a dog began to howl.

  Then came the knocking again–three slow raps, and a voice from the dark: “Let me in–”

“Don’t open it” Mabel whispered.

“Then who is it?” He asked.

Mabel pulled her shawl tighter and whispered a *prayer.

* * *

By morning, the knocking​ had stopped.
