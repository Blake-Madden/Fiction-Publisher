::: subtitle
Harbor House, the next day
:::

The storm had blown itself out before dawn, leaving the harbor flat and gray beneath a low sky.

Mabel opened the shutters one at a time, as if each might be hiding something. The porch was wet, the steps were wet, and the boards by the door were scattered with sand that had not been there the night before.

Harold came down late, the *Gazette* folded under his arm. “Well,” he said, “the house is still standing.”

“Someone was out there,” Mabel said.

He looked at the sand, and then at the road, and for once he did not tell her it was only the wind.
